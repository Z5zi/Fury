# Original harbor water surfaces

`vaultline::upgrade_world_water(scene)` runs immediately after `upgrade_world_ground` in the `FURY_WORLD_ART=1` path. This is a material treatment for the existing HarborWater, RidgeWater and NorthQuayWater planes. It adds **zero triangles, meshes, entities or colliders**, and does not move water or change its gameplay tags, visibility, LOD, vertex colors or original UVs. The original `TextureSlot::Water` remains in place so backend water/reflection routing is retained. Disabling world art retains the baseline material treatment.

## Maps and physical scale

The original in-repository generator creates one immutable 256 × 256 RGBA set shared by all three surfaces:

- Base color: sRGB-encoded dark teal-gray. Mean linear reflectance is approximately `(0.039, 0.070, 0.074)`; only ±2.5% low-frequency variation is allowed. Wave crests and foam are not painted into the color map
- Normal: linear tangent-space XYZ from analytic derivatives of twelve periodic directional waves. The patch is 16 metres wide; wavelengths span 0.63–7.16 metres, and individual amplitudes span 0.65–14 millimetres. Oblique wave directions and unequal phases avoid the former orthogonal blue/checker pattern
- Surface: linear glTF-style channels, R=255 unused, G=roughness around 0.245–0.285, B=0 metallic, A=255. Material roughness is 1 so the G channel is not unintentionally multiplied by the legacy 0.18 factor; metallic is 0, as water is dielectric

World projection uses `world_uv_scale = 1/16`, independent of the planes' legacy 8×/10× UV tiling, size, and position. UV scroll is 0.12 m/s along world X and 0.045 m/s along world Z. This is translated normal-field animation, not a fluid or dispersive-wave simulation. The underlying mesh remains perfectly flat.

The original water vertices carry a strong blue tint. A bounded per-channel inverse of the mesh's mean vertex color neutralizes it through the material, without editing any mesh data. On the three uniform-color baseline water planes the compensation is exact. New nonuniform water art should use its own authored maps instead.

All maps have eight **additional** mip levels, 128×128 down to 1×1. Color is filtered in linear light and re-encoded as sRGB, normal vectors are renormalized, and surface channels stay linear. The image helper returns level zero as well; the generator deliberately removes it from the additional-level vectors. Resident image payload is exactly **1,048,572 bytes**, shared across all three planes, excluding small container/string overhead and renderer-owned uploads/caches. No external assets, downloads or licenses are involved.

## Preservation and scope

Selection is by exact entity name, a nonempty mesh, and the existing Water slot. Other water/metal/mission materials are untouched. Existing authored/source map sets are preserved. Repeated application is a no-op on upgraded entities; partially constructed scenes can gain later water surfaces. Counts explicitly distinguish harbor, ridge and North Quay. The public map factory permits independent deterministic generation tests; runtime uses only one set per upgraded scene.

The new surface is opaque and has no emissive contribution or procedural foam. It does not claim depth-aware shoreline foam, volumetric scattering, displacement, caustics, an ocean simulation or fully converged light transport. CPU ray mode now reflects actual geometry; raster reflection approximations remain separate.

## Backend behavior and limits

- **CPU software rasterizer:** samples the generated maps with the existing world-UV, normal-map and mip pipeline. Its old Water tint/foam path already requires no material maps, so generated water bypasses it. Existing Water reflection approximation is retained when enabled
- **CPU ray tracer:** samples these color/normal/surface maps with its ray-cone mip path. Water materials with a valid normal map now bypass the pre-existing additional analytic wave perturbation. In the bounded `RayTraced` mode, opaque nonmetal glossy surfaces with roughness at most `0.35` can continue along the reflected direction, weighted by Schlick Fresnel; matte surfaces and Direct debug mode retain their early exits, and the configured bounce limit still applies. This permits actual reflected scene geometry in harbor water, rather than only ambient color. The reflected ray cone widens by `0.25 * roughness²`; it is a practical filtered mirror-direction approximation, not sampled rough microfacet transport. CPU BRDF and reflection use dielectric `F0 = ((IOR-1)/(IOR+1))²`, approximately `0.0204` for water at IOR 1.333 and `0.04` for the unchanged default IOR 1.5. Water remains opaque under these materials. At frozen time, the same mapped material with and without the Water slot produces identical normal-debug and beauty images
- **OpenGL:** a narrowly scoped guard runs the old analytic normal/tint/UV-edge-foam block only for Water materials **without a valid normal map**. The previous foam test treated every authored UV outside `[0,1]` as shoreline, even on 8×/10× tiled planes. Generated and source normal maps now retain their intended color/normals. Normal-less legacy water keeps its previous behavior. The separate Water-slot Fresnel/environment approximation is unchanged
- **DXR:** the existing packed Water lane distinguishes ordinary material (0), legacy analytic water (1), and water with a valid normal map (2), without changing the material ABI. Mapped water takes the normal-map path and keeps its authored base color, roughness, transmission and IOR; dielectric BRDF F0 also derives from IOR. Only legacy normal-less water retains the analytic-wave override, 70% base-color blend toward `(0.035,0.12,0.105)`, roughness `0.065`, transmission `0.86`, IOR `1.333`, absorption `(0.28,0.08,0.035)`, and reactive-mask minimum `0.12`. This aligns mapped-material semantics; lighting, light transport and filtering still differ by backend. Actual DXR execution requires Windows/GPU validation and is not established by CPU or source checks

The shared `make_plane` helper also corrects triangle winding to agree with its existing +Y normals while retaining vertex positions/colors and UV orientation. That engine fix prevents above-water views from being backfaces with incorrectly flipped lighting. The water module itself never edits existing geometry.

## Verification

`fury_world_water_tests` checks independently generated byte determinism, full mip dimensions/encoding, linear roughness and nonmetal channels, opaque alpha, low-amplitude unit normals, periodic seams, negative/repeated UV sampling, visible normal-field scroll, physical scale/speed, exact three-surface coverage, shared image budget, zero added geometry, unchanged mesh/vertex/collider/transform/tag/behavior data, idempotence, partial scenes regardless of entity order, and source-map preservation. Its 64×64 CPU-ray fixture checks actual horizontal-plane winding plus frozen-time normal-debug and beauty equivalence between mapped Water and the same material without the Water tag.

The engine's `cpu_ray_tracing` suite additionally places an off-camera emissive target in a glossy dielectric reflection and verifies that real geometry appears, lowering IOR to 1.333 reduces reflected energy, a matte material keeps its early exit, and Direct debug mode does not add the indirect reflection. The actual reflected loft capture visibly shows the surrounding towers, shop fronts and neon sign in the original basin, distorted by the restrained generated wave normals.

`fury_world_water_tests --gl` is a separate optional runtime check, returning 77 if actual SDL video/context creation or backend availability prevents running it. Fury loads GL entry points through SDL, so the optional CMake OpenGL-package flag is not used to infer runtime availability. Shader initialization failure after a successful context probe remains a test failure. With reflections disabled and ambient-only lighting, generated and arbitrary authored normal-mapped Water surfaces must match the identical untagged material pixel-for-pixel even with 10× tiled UVs. Normal-less water must still use its legacy treatment. A real horizontal `make_plane`, viewed from above with sunlight and no ambient, must also remain lit and match the untagged mapped material. Existing `gl_materials`, `surface_sampling`, software raster and CPU-ray tests cover the general map path. An actual GL runtime context is required to claim the guard was runtime-verified.

Full-world captures should compare identical harbor/loft, ridge and quay camera, time, weather, resolution, backend and detail settings. Inspect the newly opened basins, not a texture swatch: the result should read as restrained dark water with lighting-driven variation, without saturated blue tile grids or repeated UV-edge foam. Geometry and gameplay preservation are covered separately by the ground/world tests.
